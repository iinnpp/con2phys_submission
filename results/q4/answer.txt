Pairwise spike train interactions were estimated from single unit activity over the entire recording. All unordered pairs of
units recorded from the same mouse and the same brain area were included; units were not paired across mice or across brain
areas. This yielded 25,299 pairs from 1,449 units and 18 mice: 14,008 pairs in area 1, 7,632 in area 2, and 3,659 in area 3.

Interactions were quantified with the Spike Time Tiling Coefficient (STTC).STTC was calculated with dt = 100 ms. 
The implementation from the isttc package was used (https://github.com/iinnpp/isttc). Spike
timestamps remained as floating-point seconds and dt was therefore specified as 0.1 s; timestamps were not converted to
integer seconds or sample indices. The exported dataset contained an LFP-derived recording duration but not the start of
the recording clock. For each mouse, the observation window was therefore anchored at its earliest observed spike and
extended for the full LFP-derived recording duration. This retained all spikes, including valid negative timestamps caused
by an offset spike clock. STTC is described in Cutts, C. S., & Eglen, S. J. (2014), 
https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2767-14.2014).

STTC values were analyzed on their original scale without transformation. I fitted a linear mixed-effects model with brain
area as a fixed effect and mouse_id as a random intercept to account for pairs recorded from the same mouse. The model
showed an overall effect of brain area on STTC (F(2, 24854) = 109.32, p < 2.2 x 10^-16). Model-estimated mean STTC values
were 0.0649 for area 1 (95% CI: 0.0522-0.0775), 0.0941 for area 2 (0.0814-0.1069), and 0.0870 for area 3
(0.0739-0.1001). Those values are in the table above.

Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons were performed and are reported as differences in mean STTC. Area 1 was 0.0292 lower
than area 2 (area 1 - area 2: 95% CI: -0.0341 to -0.0244, p < 0.0001), and area 1 was 0.0221 lower than area 3
(area 1 - area 3: -0.0284 to -0.0159, p < 0.0001). Area 2 was 0.0071 higher than area 3
(area 2 - area 3: 0.0005-0.0137, p = 0.0305). Therefore, brain area 2 had the strongest pairwise spike train
interactions at the 100-ms timescale.
