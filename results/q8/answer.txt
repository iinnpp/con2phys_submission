Spike-LFP phase locking was estimated from single-unit activity over the entire recording. Each unit was paired
with the median LFP voltage trace across channels from the same mouse and brain area. This yielded 1,449 units from
18 mice, with 649 units in area 1, 478 in area 2, and 322 in area 3.

Phase locking was quantified with pairwise phase consistency (PPC), the dimensionless mean cosine of phase
differences across distinct spike pairs. The LFP was band-pass filtered at 4-10 Hz using NeuroDSP with a
zero-phase, third-order Butterworth filter. Instantaneous phase was estimated with the Hilbert transform and
sampled at the 500 Hz LFP sample nearest each spike. Spikes outside the LFP window or within one second of an LFP
segment boundary were excluded. Each unit needed at least 31 usable spikes.

PPC values were analyzed on their original scale. I averaged PPC within each mouse and brain area and fitted a
linear mixed-effects model, with brain area as a fixed effect and mouse_id as a
random intercept. Residual variance was estimated separately for each area. The reference degrees of freedom for
inference were the number of mice minus one. The overall test of brain area gave F(2, 17) = 14.38, p = 0.0002213.
Model-estimated mean PPC values were 0.000601 for area 1 (95% CI 0.000008 to 0.001194), 0.002410 for area 2 (95% CI
0.001262 to 0.003559), and 0.032883 for area 3 (95% CI 0.016477 to 0.049290). Those values are reported in teh table
above.

Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons were reported as differences in mean PPC. In the primary analysis, the area 1
minus area 2 difference was -0.001809 (95% CI -0.003164 to -0.000454, p = 0.00859), the area 1 minus area 3
difference was -0.032282 (95% CI -0.052229 to -0.012336, p = 0.00183), and the area 2 minus area 3 difference was
-0.030473 (95% CI -0.050455 to -0.010491, p = 0.00305).

As a sensitivity check, I calculated each unit's PPC separately against each same-area LFP channel and took the
median across channels. The model-estimated mean PPC values were 0.000484 for area 1, 0.002330 for area 2, and
0.044736 for area 3. Area 3 exceeded both other areas in Tukey-adjusted comparisons (p < 0.05). Therefore, brain
area 3 had the strongest spike-LFP phase locking in the 4-10 Hz range.
