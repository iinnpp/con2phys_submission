Broadband LFP power was calculated over the entire recording. For each channel, I estimated the power spectral
density (PSD) at 500 Hz using Welch's method in NeuroDSP with 4 s Hann windows, 50% overlap, and the median across
windows. I calculated PSDs from 0.1 to 150 Hz, took the pointwise median across channels within each mouse and
brain area, and integrated that spectrum from 1 to 100 Hz. The analysis included 1,862 channels from 18 mice (area
1: 453 channels; area 2: 593; area 3: 816).

I fitted a linear mixed-effects model to log10 broadband power with brain area as a fixed effect and mouse_id as a
random intercept to account for the three areas recorded in each mouse. The model showed an overall effect of
brain area (F(2, 34) = 439.85, p = 5.03e-25). Back-transformed model estimates, interpreted as geometric means in
microvolts squared, were 718.9 for area 1 (95% CI 579.4-891.9), 5,831.7 for area 2 (95% CI 4,700.3-7,235.4), and
29,337.0 for area 3 (95% CI 23,645.4-36,398.6). Those values are given in the table above.

Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons were performed on the log scale and are reported as ratios of geometric means.
Area 2 had 8.11 times the power of area 1 (95% CI 5.97-11.03, p < 0.0001). Area 3 had 40.81 times the power of
area 1 (95% CI 30.01-55.49, p < 0.0001). Area 3 had 5.03 times the power of area 2 (95% CI 3.70-6.84, p < 0.0001).
Therefore, brain area 3 had the highest broadband LFP power.
