We applied principal component analysis (PCA) separately to each brain area, pooling the 128-sample average waveforms from 
all 18 mice within each area. We mean-centered each time sample across units and retained the original waveform amplitudes. 
We applied DBSCAN and Gaussian mixture models (GMMs) to the first two and first three PC scores. For the GMMs, 
we compared one to eight components with shared or separate covariance matrices and selected the model with the 
lowest Bayesian information criterion for each area and PC count. At the reference settings, DBSCAN identified 
one dense cluster per area. The GMMs identified components with median trough-to-peak durations of 0.21–0.23 ms 
in Areas 2 and 3, while component medians in Area 1 ranged from 0.48 to 0.52 ms. We therefore treated Areas 2 and 
3 as candidates for the highest proportion of putative fast-spiking units. Their relative ranking depended on 
whether two or three PCs were included.
