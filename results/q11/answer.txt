Variable A was decoded from single-unit spiking activity during trials. Trial selection was based on the event timestamps in
the trial-information table. A trial was retained only if trial_start, stim_start, outcome, and trial_end were all present and
strictly ordered as trial_start < stim_start < outcome < trial_end. The final recorded trial for each mouse, defined as the
trial with the maximum trial_id, was excluded. The source table contained 10,373 trials from 18 mice. The completeness and
event-order criteria retained 10,361 trials; seven final trials had already failed those criteria, and excluding the remaining
11 final trials produced a dataset of 10,350 trials. 

Because variable A is binary, information about A was quantified as the cross-validated ROC-AUC of a population decoder.
For each retained trial, the feature for each neuron was its mean firing rate within the analyzed trial interval. Decoding
was performed separately for each mouse and brain area using an L2-regularized logistic regression. Features were
standardized within each training fold, and class weights were balanced to account for unequal numbers of A = 0 and A = 1
trials. Five-fold stratified cross-validation was performed within each mouse, using the same trial folds for all three brain
areas. To prevent an area with more recorded units from receiving an advantage, the three areas were matched within each
mouse to the smallest recorded area population. Units were randomly subsampled 20 times, and ROC-AUC was averaged across
the neuron subsamples. Mouse-level mean ROC-AUC was used as the independent observation for area-level summaries and
statistical analysis.

The decoder was first applied to the entire trial, from trial_start to trial_end. Mean ROC-AUC values were 0.722 for area 1
(95% CI: 0.672-0.772), 0.729 for area 2 (0.676-0.783), and 0.757 for area 3 (0.707-0.807), showing that variable A
could be decoded from whole-trial spiking activity. To determine when this information was most prominent, population
firing-rate time courses were then plotted separately for A = 0 and A = 1 trials. Trials were piecewise normalized into
20 equal bins for trial_start-to-stimulus,
stimulus-to-outcome, and outcome-to-trial_end, thereby aligning stimulus and outcome despite differences in trial duration.
The largest descriptive separation between A = 0 and A = 1 occurred after the outcome.

The same decoder analysis was subsequently repeated separately for all three trial portions. Mean ROC-AUC values for
trial_start-to-stimulus were 0.507 for area 1 (95% CI: 0.493-0.521), 0.528 for area 2 (0.509-0.547), and 0.501 for
area 3 (0.479-0.524). For stimulus-to-outcome they were 0.576 for area 1 (0.546-0.605), 0.594 for area 2
(0.562-0.626), and 0.607 for area 3 (0.573-0.641). For outcome-to-trial_end they were 0.818 for area 1
(0.773-0.863), 0.812 for area 2 (0.760-0.864), and 0.866 for area 3 (0.830-0.902). These descriptive 95% confidence
intervals were calculated across the 18 mouse-level mean ROC-AUC values. Thus, information about variable A was weak before
the stimulus, increased between stimulus and outcome, and was strongest after the outcome.

For the outcome-to-trial_end interval, above-chance decoding was tested using exact one-sided mouse-level sign-flip tests.
All 18 mice had a mean ROC-AUC greater than 0.5 in every brain area. Decoding was above chance in area 1 (mean ROC-AUC
0.818, p = 3.81 x 10^-6), area 2 (0.812, p = 3.81 x 10^-6), and area 3 (0.866, p = 3.81 x 10^-6).

Differences among brain areas were tested with a linear mixed-effects model using brain area as a fixed effect and mouse_id
as a random intercept. The model showed an overall effect of brain area on outcome-to-trial_end ROC-AUC
(F(2, 34) = 3.60, p = 0.0381). Model-estimated mean ROC-AUC values were 0.818 for area 1
(95% CI: 0.771-0.864), 0.812 for area 2 (0.765-0.859), and 0.866 for area 3 (0.819-0.913).

Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons did not show a significant difference between any two areas. The area 1 - area 2
difference was 0.0058 (95% CI: -0.0484 to 0.0599, p = 0.9632), the area 1 - area 3 difference was -0.0482
(-0.1023 to 0.0059, p = 0.0887), and the area 2 - area 3 difference was -0.0540
(-0.1081 to 0.0002, p = 0.0508). Therefore, variable A was strongly represented in post-outcome spiking activity in all
three areas. Area 3 had the highest descriptive decoding accuracy, but it was not significantly better than areas 1 or 2
after Tukey correction.
