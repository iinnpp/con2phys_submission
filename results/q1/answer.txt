Firing rate was calculated for each unit over the entire recording as the total number of spikes divided by the recording duration. 
Recording duration was derived from the length of the corresponding LFP signal. All 1,449 units from 18 mice were included (area 1: 649 units; 
area 2: 478; area 3: 322).

Because unit-level firing rates were strictly positive and strongly right-skewed, firing rate was log-transformed before statistical analysis. 
I fitted a linear mixed-effects model with brain area as a fixed effect and mouse_id as a random intercept to account for units recorded from the same mouse. 
The model showed an overall effect of brain area on log firing rate (p < 2.2 x 10^-16). Back-transformed model estimates, 
interpreted as geometric mean firing rates, were 5.98 Hz for area 1 (95% CI: 5.31-6.74), 3.33 Hz for area 2 (2.93-3.78), and 4.44 Hz for area 3 (3.87-5.11).
Those values are in the table above.

Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons were performed on the log scale and are reported as ratios of geometric means. Area 1 was 1.80 times higher than area 2 
(95% CI: 1.57-2.06, p < 0.0001), area 1 was 1.35 times higher than area 3 (1.15-1.57, p < 0.0001), and area 2 was 0.75 times area 3 (0.64-0.88, p = 0.0001). 
Therefore, brain area 2 had the lowest firing rate.

The analysis of untransformed firing rates led to the same conclusion that area 2 had the lowest firing rate, although the difference between areas 1 and 3 
was not statistically significant.
