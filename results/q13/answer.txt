Population dimensionality was examined using single-unit spiking activity from stimulus presentation to outcome. Trial
selection used exactly the same filtered trial table as Q11 and Q12. A trial was retained only if trial_start, stim_start,
outcome, and trial_end were all present and strictly ordered as trial_start < stim_start < outcome < trial_end. The final
recorded trial for each mouse, defined as the trial with the maximum trial_id, was excluded. The source table contained
10,373 trials from 18 mice. The completeness and event-order criteria retained 10,361 trials; seven final trials had already
failed those criteria, and excluding the remaining 11 final trials produced a dataset of 10,350 trials.

For each retained trial and neuron, activity was represented by the mean firing rate in the half-open interval from
stim_start to outcome, [stim_start, outcome). Spike count was divided by the actual interval duration for each trial. This
produced one trial-by-neuron firing-rate matrix for every mouse and brain area. Each neuron's firing rates were centered and
standardized across trials before principal component analysis (PCA), so the dimensionality estimate reflected population
covariation rather than differences in absolute firing-rate scale. Neurons with no trial-to-trial variance were represented
by zeros after standardization.

Dimensionality was quantified primarily by the PCA participation ratio, (sum(lambda))^2 / sum(lambda^2), where lambda denotes
the PCA eigenvalues. This measure gives the effective number of dimensions contributing to the population activity. To
prevent an area with more recorded neurons from having a higher possible dimensionality simply because it contained more
units, the three brain areas were matched separately within each mouse to the size of that mouse's smallest recorded area
population. The matched population sizes ranged from 5 to 32 neurons. Units were randomly subsampled 100 times, and the
participation ratio was averaged across subsamples. The resulting mouse-level mean for each brain area was used as the
independent observation for statistical inference.

Differences among brain areas were tested with a linear mixed-effects model using brain area as a fixed effect and mouse_id
as a random intercept. The model showed an overall effect of brain area on participation ratio
(F(2, 34) = 5.88, p = 0.0064). Model-estimated mean participation ratios were 11.48 for area 1
(95% CI: 9.46-13.50), 9.32 for area 2 (7.30-11.34), and 9.21 for area 3 (7.20-11.23). Those values are given in teh table above.

Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons showed that dimensionality was higher in area 1 than in both other areas. The area 1
minus area 2 difference was 2.16 (95% CI: 0.33-3.99, p = 0.0175), and the area 1 minus area 3 difference was 2.27
(0.44-4.10, p = 0.0123). Areas 2 and 3 did not differ: the area 2 minus area 3 difference was 0.11
(-1.72 to 1.93, p = 0.9889). 

Therefore, neural activity from stimulus presentation to outcome had the highest dimensionality in brain area 1.
