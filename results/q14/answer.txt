Modularity was estimated from single-unit spikes across the entire recording, including the intervals
between trials. I reused the pairwise STTC values from Q4. For each
mouse, Q4 used a window starting at the earliest recorded spike and extending for the full LFP-derived
recording duration. Two spikes counted as close in time if they occurred within 100 ms of each other.
Only pairs recorded within the same mouse and area were used.

To compare networks of the same size within each mouse, I matched all three areas to the neuron count
in that mouse's smallest recorded area. Four of 18 mice had fewer than 10 neurons in at least one area.
This left 14 mice with matched sets of 10 to 32 neurons per area. For each mouse and area, we drew 100
random neuron sets, sampling without replacement within each set. The smallest area contributed all its
neurons each time. In each network, the neurons were the nodes and the links were pairs with the highest
positive STTC values. The number of links was 20% of all possible pairs among the selected neurons,
rounded up to a whole number. I used the original STTC values as link weights, so stronger spike-time
associations contributed more to the score.

Modularity (Q) measures how clearly neurons form groups with strong links within each group. For a given
grouping, Q compares the amount of link weight inside the groups with the amount expected from each
neuron's total link weight. A higher Q means that more of the network's weight is concentrated within
groups than expected. I used weighted Leiden community detection at resolution gamma = 1 to find groups
and kept the highest Q from 25 runs with different random seeds.

Even networks with randomly arranged links can yield a positive Q when the grouping is chosen to
maximize it. To estimate that baseline, I made 20 randomized versions of every observed network. I
swapped which neurons were linked while keeping the number of links per neuron fixed, then shuffled the
original STTC weights across those links. This kept the same number of neurons and links and the same
set of weights. I optimized Q separately for each randomized network at the same resolution, using 10
runs per network. Chance-corrected modularity (delta Q) was the observed Q minus the mean Q of the 20
randomized networks. A positive delta Q indicates stronger grouping than the randomized baseline, and a
value near zero indicates a similar amount of grouping. I averaged delta Q across the 100 neuron sets
to obtain one score for each mouse and area. The mouse was the independent unit.

I compared the three areas using a linear mixed-effects model with brain area as a fixed effect and
mouse as a random intercept to account for repeated measurements from each mouse. The overall area
comparison gave F(2, 39) = 1.58, p = 0.2180. Model-estimated mean delta Q was 0.0242 for area 1 (95% CI
-0.0015 to 0.0499), 0.0398 for area 2 (95% CI 0.0141 to 0.0655), and 0.0562 for area 3 (95% CI 0.0305 to
0.0819). Those values are reported in the table above.

I adjusted the three pairwise comparisons using Tukey's method. The area 1 minus area 2 difference was
-0.0156 (95% CI -0.0594 to 0.0282, p = 0.6637). The area 1 minus area 3 difference was -0.0320 (95% CI
-0.0758 to 0.0118, p = 0.1896). The area 2 minus area 3 difference was -0.0164 (95% CI -0.0602 to
0.0274, p = 0.6359).

The random-intercept variance was estimated at zero. As a robustness check, a permutation test
that preserved the pairing within each mouse was done. Shuffling the area labels within mice 100,000 times gave p
= 0.2692.

To check how much the ranking depended on how the networks were constructed and scored, I varied the
link density (10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and 30% at gamma = 1) and the resolution parameter (gamma = 0.5, 0.75,
1, 1.25, 1.5, and 2 at 20% density). The resolution parameter controls the scale of the groups the score
favors. Each additional setting used 20 neuron sets and 10 randomized networks per set. Brain area 1 had
the lowest mean delta Q across all 10 settings, including the primary setting.

Brain area 1 had the lowest estimated modularity but the Tukey-adjusted comparisons
provided insufficient evidence that it was lower than both other areas.
