Pairwise spike train interactions were estimated from single-unit activity over the entire recording.
All unordered pairs of simultaneously recorded units from different brain areas within the same mouse
were included. This yielded 39,325 pairs from 1,449 units and 18 mice, with 18,560 for areas 1 and 2,
11,598 for areas 1 and 3, and 9,167 for areas 2 and 3.

Interactions were quantified using the Spike Time Tiling Coefficient (STTC) with dt = 100 ms. I used
the same vectorized isttc implementation as in Q4 (https://github.com/iinnpp/isttc). Spike timestamps
were kept in floating-point seconds, with dt specified as 0.1 s. For each mouse, the observation window
began at the earliest observed spike and extended for the full LFP-derived recording duration, retaining
valid negative timestamps. STTC is described in Cutts, C. S., & Eglen, S. J. (2014)
(https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2767-14.2014).

STTC values were analyzed on their original scale. The linear mixed-effects model included area pair as
a fixed effect and a random intercept for mouse. The Satterthwaite test for the overall area-pair effect
gave F(2, 38834.8) = 437.94, p < 2.2e-16. The model-estimated mean STTC values were 0.0465 for area pair
1-2 (95% CI 0.0398 to 0.0531), 0.0097 for area pair 1-3 (95% CI 0.0029 to 0.0164), and 0.0227 for area
pair 2-3 (95% CI 0.0159 to 0.0295).

Tukey-adjusted pairwise comparisons gave differences in mean STTC of 0.0368 for (1-2) - (1-3) (95% CI
0.0338 to 0.0398, p < 0.0001), 0.0238 for (1-2) - (2-3) (95% CI 0.0206 to 0.0271, p < 0.0001), and
-0.0130 for (1-3) - (2-3) (95% CI -0.0165 to -0.0095, p < 0.0001). Therefore, areas 1 and 2 had the
strongest undirected functional connectivity at the 100-ms timescale.
